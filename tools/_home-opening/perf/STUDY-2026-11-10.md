# Long garage: speed options, ranked for Nov 19

Dan, this is the final ranking for the homepage room (2026-11-10).

- **What was measured:** the pinned version 3020b60, which has 23 model files, on your Mac (M1 Max). Newer versions were re-checked where noted.
- **Rule for gains:** a speed gain counts only where both independent timing lanes agree. Anything else is marked **not confirmed**.

**Bottom line:** nothing loads or gets built in the middle of the walk. It feels stuck for two reasons:
- Scrolls made while the room is still loading are saved up, and then the room glides you to the middle and stops there.
- The heaviest pictures land where the camera moves fastest, and that shows as jumps whenever your Mac is busy.

Three fixes keep today's look exactly and fit before Nov 19: O1, O4 and O3-safe, about 7 h together. The slow-internet fixes (O9, O8) need a quick look or a yes from you. The two big speed-ups (O2, O7) change the look.

## 1. What's happening, in plain words

### Why it gets stuck in the middle

- **Nothing new loads or gets built during the walk.** After the first picture there were 0 shader builds, 0 texture uploads and 0 reflection or shadow redraws. That held across more than 127,000 measured frames in Chrome and in Safari's engine [E1]. So loading more in advance would not help.
- **Scrolling before the 3D is ready.**
  - For the first seconds the page shows a still picture of the door that looks live. Scrolls made then do nothing, so it looks stuck.
  - Those scrolls are saved up. When the 3D starts, it glides you to roughly the middle of the walk and stops there, with no headline.
  - On the current room, a scroll made during loading left it stopped at 59 % of the walk [E2]. O8 fixes this.
- **The middle is where slow pictures show.**
  - The first half of the walk is the heaviest view: the whole garage in frame, 12 lamps that light every pixel, 5 shadows, and a wet floor that draws the whole room a second time.
  - The camera moves fastest exactly in the middle: 30-43 cm per 1 % of scroll between 0.3 and 0.55, up to 2.4x its average [E3].
  - So one late picture there becomes a jump of half a metre to two metres.
- **Your Mac is often busy while you test.**
  - While we measured, other programs (Ableton, the iPhone Simulator, Blender, other browsers and agents) used 18-100 % of the graphics chip.
  - At your window size, in Chrome:
    - quiet Mac: **0** pauses over 50 ms per walk (now and then up to 5);
    - busy Mac: **32-40** pauses per walk, the worst 0.1-0.17 s [E4].
  - In Safari's engine: about 3 per run when quiet, 130-170 when busy [E1].
- **Even on a quiet Mac, the first half is not quite 60 pictures a second.**
  - At your size the door view takes 20 ms to draw; one screen refresh is 16.7 ms. So it runs at roughly 40-50 pictures a second and can judder slightly where the camera is fastest.
  - The same-look fixes bring it to about 19 ms. Only O2 gets it under 16.7 ms while walking, by drawing slightly softer while the camera moves.
- **Safari only:** every time you scroll back up from the TV there is one freeze of 50-100 ms. After a fast flick it lands in the middle of the walk (18 of 18 tries) [E5]. O6 trims it by about a fifth.

### Will a weaker computer manage it?

- **Your Mac is a top-end machine.** Even on it, the door view takes:
  - **20 ms** per picture at your window size (about 50 a second);
  - **30 ms** on a 1440x900 screen, the default of a 13-inch MacBook Air (33 a second) [E6].
- **Most of the cost is lighting work for every pixel.** Turning all 12 lamps off would make it 59 % faster, the 4x edge smoothing costs about 20 % and the shadows about 17 % [E6, E7]. Tidying the code barely touches this.
- **Weaker graphics chip:** a typical MacBook Air has roughly a quarter to a third of your Mac's graphics power. That figure comes from published specs; we did not measure one.
  - Very roughly, that means 8-15 pictures a second at the door on such a laptop (estimate).
  - The same-look fixes give only 5-9 % there.
- **Weaker processor** (simulated as 4x slower): the ~750 drawing commands per picture hold the first half to about 29 pictures a second. O3 cuts the time per picture there by a fifth to a quarter (confirmed).
- **Graphics memory:** the room keeps about 760-950 MB on the graphics chip, and more with each model. O9 cuts 115 MB. That gets tight on 8 GB machines as more models land (estimate).
- **Making entry-level laptops truly smooth** means a lighter look on those machines only: O2 while walking, or a trimmed O7. That changes the look there, so it needs your eye.
- **Caution: no real weak laptop has been measured yet.** This is the biggest open question before Nov 19.

### Slow internet

- **Slow internet only delays the start.** Nothing downloads during the walk, and returning visitors download nothing.
- **The walk waits for every file.** At the tested version (16 MB compressed) [E8]:

  | Line speed | Still picture shows | Walk can start |
  |---|---|---|
  | 4 Mbps | ~1.5 s | ~35-39 s |
  | 1.6 Mbps | ~2.7 s | ~83-87 s |

  That is about 2 s per MB at 4 Mbps and about 5 s per MB at 1.6 Mbps.
- **Today's room is bigger:** 82 model files, about **22 MB** compressed (measured from the files today).
  - So the walk starts after roughly **50 s** at 4 Mbps and **2 minutes** at 1.6 Mbps (estimate).
  - Each new model adds to that.
- **What the fixes buy** (estimates at the tested version):
  - O9 (smaller texture files, -2.8 MB): the walk starts ~6 s sooner at 4 Mbps and ~14 s sooner at 1.6 Mbps.
  - O8 (download everything at once): ~2-3 s sooner.
  - Together with O1: about 27 s at 4 Mbps and 64 s at 1.6 Mbps.
- **Blocker: without O1's Cloudflare change, the live site blocks the pictures on every model.** The models show white, and the REVAUDIO and ON AIR signs fall back to a thin font [E9].
- **The bigger fix for slow lines is a product choice, not a speed fix:** let visitors scroll the picture like a normal page until the 3D is ready. That is a separate question for you.

**What the package buys**
- **Your Mac at your size:** about 5-7 % less graphics time per picture from O4, plus about 2 % from O3. They were not timed together.
- **Slow-processor laptops:** each picture in the first half of the walk is a fifth to a quarter quicker.
  - This was measured on the full O3. The safe version keeps the main part, which is about four-fifths of the processor saving.
  - That part gets more valuable with every model: on a newer version (11-10) it removes 424 shader re-checks per picture, up from 284 at the tested version.

**What it doesn't do**
- It won't stop the jumps that come from other programs using your Mac's graphics chip.
- It barely shortens the slow-internet wait; that is O9 and O8.
- It won't make entry-level laptops smooth; that needs a lighter look on them.
- It won't get your door view to a full 60 pictures a second: about 19 ms per picture, against the 16.7 ms needed.

**How to land it**
- **O1, today.** In Cloudflare's site-wide security rule, add `blob:` in two places:
  - `img-src 'self' data: https:` becomes `img-src 'self' data: blob: https:`
  - `connect-src 'self' https://api.paddle.com ...` becomes `connect-src 'self' blob: https://api.paddle.com ...`
  - Make the edit by hand. Don't paste a whole new policy, because others edit that rule too.
  - Then check the live header and do one Paddle checkout.
  - In the room, ship the version without the 5 preload links: they delay the still picture by 0.3-0.9 s on slow lines. Self-host Lenis (the smooth-scroll script) and the fonts, and drop the unused meshopt import.
- **O4 and O3-safe, by Nov 13**, before the Nov 14-17 homepage integration. They go in through the session that owns the worktree.
  - Add O4's two safety checks: fall back to today's path if the glow code isn't where it is expected, and stop adding glow if the glow is ever switched off.
  - Re-shoot the 7 stills at DPR 1 and 2, plus Safari's engine, on the final room.
- **O3-safe** has four parts:
  - draw the floor reflection before the main picture, not from inside it;
  - draw the sign in one pass;
  - hide outlines that are invisible anyway;
  - leave the floor and the soft contact shadows out of the reflection.

  It leaves out O3's "small things" rule, because that rule made a chrome stool stand disappear from the wet floor [E12].
  - Before landing it, re-check walking back up at the door on your Mac. Lane 1 saw a worse worst frame there (0.23 s against 0.15-0.17 s); lane 2 could not reproduce it in 5 sessions.
- **Caution, homepage port (Nov 14-17):** reuse the site's own Lenis. O1's note to "import Lenis" would start a second one, and two smoothers make the walk jerky or stuck. Keep the site's fonts.css, and drop meshopt there too.

- **Gain:**
  - Exact counts: 2.80 MB less to download (-17 %) and 115 MB less graphics memory (-19.5 %).

  - In very wide windows (21:9, 3440x1440), the amp cabinet's vinyl grain at the left edge goes slightly softer around walk 0.5 [E10].
  - In Safari's engine at your size, one floor-reflection blob is ~6 % brighter. You would only see it by flipping between the two versions.
  - Fix: keep the amp's 3 tolex textures at full size (+0.24 MB). Everywhere else it is within noise.
- **Your OK:** committing the resized model and texture files, plus a folder of full-size originals.
- **When:** as the last asset step at the freeze (Nov 16-17).
  - Build it fresh from the full-size originals, after 4 fixes to the build kit.
  - Don't commit either prebuilt set: the one built for 3020b60 is already out of date on today's room (the couch blanket).
  - About 7 h.

### O8 · Download everything at once and always open at the door: do it if you OK the behaviour
- **Gain:**
  - The room opens at the door even if someone scrolled while it loaded, instead of gliding to the middle and stopping there (exact).
  - Walk start, measured once: 2.2 s sooner at 4 Mbps and 2.7 s sooner at 1.6 Mbps.
  - On a slow-processor laptop, the longest frozen moment at start drops from 3.4 s to under 0.9 s.
  - Frame time is unchanged in both lanes.
- **Look:** the room at rest is bit-identical, but what visitors see while it loads changes:
  - it opens at the door;
  - screens wider than 1141 px get the right-size nav bar from the start;
  - the CRT screens flicker on a different random schedule [E14], fixable with a fixed seed;
  - pressing End or dragging the scrollbar during loading also jumps back to the door, fixable by resetting only when the visitor is still inside the walk.
- **Your OK:** the open-at-the-door behaviour.
- **When:** land it by Nov 12-13, with 4 small fixes:
  - a time-out on the TV picture (today the start could hang forever if that picture never arrives);
  - off-thread picture decoding only in Chrome and Firefox;
  - keep its screen-size code inside the room's module for the homepage port;
  - reset to the door only when the visitor is still inside the walk.

  About 6 h.

### O2 · Lighter while walking, sharp when standing still: only if you like it in motion
- **Gain (confirmed):**
  - While walking, each picture is 21.5 % cheaper on sharp (2x) screens in lane 1 and 24-28 % cheaper in lane 2.
  - Your door view drops to about 15 ms, under the 16.7 ms that a full 60 pictures a second needs.
  - Walking smoothness is 8-23 % better (lane 2).
  - Nothing changes on 1x screens or for slow processors.
- **Look: visible.**
  - While moving, the TV text, the neon letters and fine textures soften.
  - When the camera stops, the picture sharpens over 0.15 s and some highlights blink, such as the white box's top edge and the car's trunk glint [E11].
  - Standing still, it is exactly today's picture.
- **Your OK:** judge it in motion in your own Safari. The video and a live copy are ready.
- About 5.5 h.

## 7. How sure we are

- **The Mac was busy during every measurement.** Load averages ran from 7 to 830, and other agents often kept the graphics chip 95-100 % busy.
  - Raw times are inflated (3-4x in lane 1), so this report relies only on paired comparisons, counts and pixel differences.
  - Lane 2 found quiet stretches whose baselines matched the earlier quiet baseline within 0.3 ms.
- **Real Safari was never measured.** "Safari" here means Safari's engine (WebKit) driven by a test harness on this Mac. O6's target and O2's look need your real Safari.
- **No weak laptop was measured.** A 4x CPU slow-down stands in for a weak processor. The weak-graphics numbers are estimates from published specs.
- **Load-time gains (O1, O8, O9) were measured once each,** by their prototypes (2-4 interleaved pairs). The two timing lanes covered frame time only. O9's byte and memory savings are exact counts.
- **The lanes disagree on O3.**
  - With no slow-down, lane 1 measured +5.0 % and lane 2 measured +1.5-1.9 %.
  - Lane 1's door-end worst-frame regression (0.23 s against 0.15-0.17 s, 13 of 14 passes) was not reproduced in lane 2's 5 sessions. It is still open.
- **Every timing is from 3020b60, and today's room is heavier.** It has 82 model files instead of 23. At the door, draw calls are up 12 % (757 to 851) and triangles up 71 %. It was not re-timed.
  - Every option must be re-applied and re-checked on the final room.
  - O3's small-things rule and O7's light cut have already broken on newer versions.
- **One lead is still open:** a single pause on the very first scroll after the room appears, 0.03-0.07 s on a quiet Mac and 0.2-0.3 s on a busy one. The cause was not found.
- **The CSP fix was tested only as a header on a local server.** Verify it on a deployed preview in Chrome, Safari and Firefox.
- **The quality checks froze the clock or used reduced motion** so that frames could be compared pixel for pixel. Your eye in motion is still the final test for O2, O8 and O9.

1. The package does not fix the reported stutter, and the fixes the evidence points to were never prototyped.
- The final names three causes:
  (a) scrolls made during loading are saved up, then the room glides to mid-walk and stops;
  (b) late frames where the camera moves fastest;
  (c) Safari's freeze when leaving the TV.
- O1+O4+O3-safe fixes neither (a) nor (c). It trims (b) by about 5-7 % of GPU time in Chrome (door about 19 ms, against the 16.7 ms a 60 Hz screen needs). Only O8 fixes (a), and O8 is rank 5, 6 h, visibly changes loading, and needs Dan's OK.

  - A flatter walk curve. ease 2.8 and feel 0.4 (room-head.html:210) are already bench sliders (room-head.html:2469-2470). The camera peaks at 43.1 cm per 1 % of scroll at p0.5, 2.4x the walk's average (m-audit). This costs nothing to render, but it changes the feel, so it needs Dan's eye.

- Compression:
  - R2 does not gzip on the fly, and Cloudflare's default compression list very likely omits model/gltf-binary. So .glb would ship raw.
